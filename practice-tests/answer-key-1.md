**Q1. Correct: D**

This replaces an output metric (features shipped) with an outcome metric (user activation), directly measuring the value delivered to customers rather than the volume of work produced.

**Q2. Correct: A**

Continuous delivery aligns with the ongoing nature of the digital product life cycle, reduces launch risk, and enables faster learning loops based on real usage data rather than a one-time launch event.

---

**Q3. Correct: D**

The digital PM role is continuous and spans the full life cycle end-to-end, owning validated discovery, cross-functional delivery, and ongoing growth activities rather than being confined to a single phase.

---

**Q4. Correct: A**

Platform and data products are first-class digital products with internal 'customers' (consuming teams), requiring ongoing product management, roadmapping, and reliability/SLA ownership just like any external-facing product.

---

**Q5. Correct: A**

The defining characteristic of the project-to-product shift is moving to persistent, cross-functional teams that own a product long-term and are accountable for ongoing outcomes, rather than temporary teams disbanded after a fixed deliverable.

---

**Q6. Correct: A**

The core CDPM distinction is that digital products operate on a continuous, iterative life cycle where discovery, delivery, and growth recur indefinitely, rather than concluding at a single launch as with physical products.

---

**Q7. Correct: C**

When the problem itself is undefined, qualitative semi-structured interviews (the empathize stage of Design Thinking) are the right method to surface the underlying jobs, motivations, and pain points before any solution or metric can be meaningfully defined.

---

**Q8. Correct: C**

The social job dimension captures how a customer wants to be perceived by others when using a product or service — in this case, signaling status to colleagues.

---

**Q9. Correct: A**

Job stories follow the structure 'When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]' — this format grounds the need in a specific context rather than a persona label, which is the point of the JTBD reformulation of user stories.

---

**Q10. Correct: B**

Design Thinking is intentionally sequenced so that empathize and define validate the problem space before ideation and prototyping begin. Skipping these stages risks solving a problem that doesn't actually exist for users, wasting build effort regardless of execution quality.

---

**Q11. Correct: A**

The opportunity-solution tree structure flows from outcome at the top, to opportunities (unmet needs, pain points, desires) as the second layer, then solutions beneath opportunities, and assumption tests/experiments beneath solutions.

---

**Q12. Correct: B**

Continuous discovery habits emphasize a weekly cadence of small, consistent customer touchpoints (e.g., interviews) rather than large periodic research sprints, keeping the team continuously connected to evolving user needs.

---

**Q13. Correct: D**

The opportunity-solution tree (Teresa Torres' continuous discovery habit) starts with a desired outcome at the root, maps customer opportunities (needs, pain points, desires) as branches, and connects them to solutions and experiments — directly addressing the need to link scattered qualitative insight to prioritized, outcome-aligned opportunities.

---

**Q14. Correct: C**

Job stories follow the structure "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]," and JTBD theory distinguishes functional jobs (the task itself) from emotional jobs (how the customer wants to feel). The phrase "without worrying I missed something" already captures the emotional job — reassurance/peace of mind — so the story is already complete; no rewrite is needed.

---

**Q15. Correct: B**

A landing page (smoke test) MVP measures revealed preference — actual behavior like clicking 'Get Started' and entering payment details — at minimal build cost. It directly tests willingness to pay, which is the riskiest assumption, without building any real product.

---

**Q16. Correct: A**

In a Wizard of Oz MVP, the customer believes the product is fully automated while humans perform the work manually behind the curtain — this tests demand for the automated experience without building the automation.

---

**Q17. Correct: D**

A p-value of 0.08 exceeds the common 0.05 significance threshold, and two days is typically insufficient sample size/duration to account for variance. Stopping early on marginal results ('peeking') is a classic cause of false-positive conclusions in experimentation.

---

**Q18. Correct: A**

Validated learning is defined by Eric Ries as insight derived from empirically testing a specific hypothesis through experiments measuring actual customer behavior, not opinion or assumption — it is the core unit of progress in Lean Startup.

---

**Q19. Correct: C**

Lean Startup treats the value hypothesis as foundational: if customers don't find core value, growth built on top of it is unsustainable regardless of early referral signals. Repeated invalidation of the value hypothesis is the textbook trigger to pivot the product direction rather than persevere.

---

**Q20. Correct: C**

The defining trait of an MVP is that it is an experiment with a clear hypothesis, built with minimum effort to maximize validated learning. A release built for nine months on assumption, with no hypothesis or prior validation, is simply a mislabeled full first release — regardless of scope.

---

**Q21. Correct: D**

The riskiest assumption is willingness to PAY for the curated experience itself, not general interest. A Wizard of Oz MVP delivers the actual experience manually behind an automated-looking front end, letting the team observe real payment and renewal behavior without building the engine — the cheapest test that still produces valid learning about paid demand.

---

**Q22. Correct: B**

An MVP's defining trait is minimizing engineering investment while still generating valid learning about the riskiest assumption. Investing in enterprise-grade scalability, uptime SLAs, and polished tooling before the underlying hypothesis is validated is a hallmark of a mislabeled first release masquerading as an MVP.

---

**Q23. Correct: B**

WSJF = Cost of Delay / Job Duration (job size). Feature A = 40/5 = 8; Feature B = 30/3 = 10. Even though Feature A has a higher absolute Cost of Delay, WSJF normalizes for the size of the job, and Feature B delivers more value per unit of time, so it should be sequenced first.

---

**Q24. Correct: D**

Now-Next-Later roadmaps organize work by confidence and desired outcomes (Now = actively being delivered with high confidence, Next = validated and upcoming, Later = exploratory ideas) rather than fixed dates, which communicates direction while allowing the plan to adapt as learning occurs — ideal for high-uncertainty digital products.

---

**Q25. Correct: A**

RICE score = (Reach x Impact x Confidence) / Effort = (1,000 x 2 x 0.8) / 4 = 1,600 / 4 = 400. Confidence must be expressed as a decimal (80% = 0.8) before multiplying, and the product is then divided by effort.

---

**Q26. Correct: C**

Best practice for a PM managing technical debt is to negotiate a recurring capacity allocation within each sprint (a common benchmark is 15-20%) so debt is continuously addressed alongside feature delivery, and to make this tradeoff transparent to stakeholders using data on velocity and defect trends rather than letting debt accumulate silently or halting all feature work.

---

**Q27. Correct: B**

Basic (must-be) requirements in the Kano model are baseline expectations: their absence causes strong dissatisfaction, but their presence does not increase satisfaction because customers simply assume they will be there — viewing an account balance in a banking app is a classic must-be feature.

---

**Q28. Correct: C**

The story bundles two large, unrelated pieces of functionality (checkout redesign and account management) into one item with a vague, unverifiable goal ('better'), violating both the 'Small' criterion (it should be sized to fit within an iteration) and 'Testable' criterion (there's no way to write concrete acceptance criteria for 'better'). It needs to be split into smaller, independently testable stories.

---

**Q29. Correct: A**

WSJF sequences work by economic efficiency: Cost of Delay divided by Job Duration (job size). Dividing by duration favors items that deliver high value relative to how long they take to complete, maximizing the rate of value delivery across the backlog. Items are ranked from highest score to lowest.

---

**Q30. Correct: D**

A Now-Next-Later roadmap communicates direction using outcome-oriented themes grouped by relative time horizon instead of fixed dates. This preserves flexibility to adapt as experiment data comes in, while still giving stakeholders a clear sense of strategic priority order.

---

**Q31. Correct: B**

Cohort analysis exists precisely to reveal that aggregate metrics like flat WAU can mask divergent behavior between acquisition sources; the correct next action is to reallocate spend toward the higher-quality channel and diagnose the root cause of paid-search churn (e.g., activation friction, poor fit) before scaling further.

---

**Q32. Correct: D**

A North Star metric must capture the moment the product delivers its core value to both sides of the marketplace; completed paid transactions reflect actual value exchange, are outcome-oriented, and align product, growth, and revenue teams around a single leading indicator of business health.

---

**Q33. Correct: B**

Funnel analysis is used to localize the single highest-leverage point of friction; with one step showing a disproportionately large drop relative to all others, best practice is to concentrate diagnostic effort (session recordings, user interviews, A/B tests) on that specific step rather than the whole flow.

---

**Q34. Correct: D**

The core distinction taught in digital product management is that vanity metrics (e.g., total downloads, page views) can trend upward without revealing why or what to do next, whereas actionable metrics are connected to a specific input the team controls, enabling a clear decision or experiment.

---

**Q35. Correct: D**

Proper experiment design pairs a primary success metric with predefined guardrail metrics that must not regress; for a checkout flow, average order value and refund/cancellation rate are directly at risk, and thresholds should be set before the test starts so the team can stop early if the business is being harmed, even if the primary metric improves.

---

**Q36. Correct: A**

A sudden influx of viral, lower-intent signups will naturally dilute the free-to-paid conversion rate denominator even if the original user base's conversion behavior is unchanged; the correct diagnostic step is to segment conversion by acquisition cohort/source to confirm whether this is a mix-shift effect rather than a genuine monetization problem, before making structural pricing changes.

---

**Q37. Correct: C**

A retention curve that flattens rather than continuing to decay toward zero indicates a stable, engaged core of users who have found the product's core value ('smile curve' pattern). The correct growth lever is to improve activation/onboarding so more of each new cohort converts into that stable core, rather than just adding volume at the top of the funnel or assuming the product has no product-market fit.

---

**Q38. Correct: C**

A PQL score is purpose-built for PLG monetization: it converts behavioral usage signals (depth of feature adoption, team invites, usage frequency) into a lead-prioritization signal that correlates with purchase intent, letting sales focus on the accounts already realizing value rather than cold-calling the entire free-tier base.

---

**Q39. Correct: B**

WCAG 2.1 AA is the widely adopted baseline for public and government digital products, balancing comprehensive coverage with feasibility; modern Section 508 requirements incorporate WCAG 2.1 AA success criteria directly.

---

**Q40. Correct: C**

Probabilistic ML systems must be evaluated statistically (e.g., precision, recall, satisfaction scores) against a representative dataset rather than a deterministic pass/fail, and paired with ongoing drift monitoring since real-world data shifts over time.

---

**Q41. Correct: B**

When patterns are well-understood and stable, deterministic rules are simpler to implement, explain, and audit than ML; ML should be reserved for genuinely ambiguous or evolving patterns where explicit rules would be brittle.

---

**Q42. Correct: B**

Responsible AI practice calibrates automation thresholds to risk: higher-consequence categories (e.g., self-harm content) warrant more conservative thresholds that route more cases to human review, balancing efficiency with trust and safety obligations.

---

**Q43. Correct: D**

A design system's core value is a library of reusable, tested components and patterns that speed delivery and keep experiences visually and behaviorally consistent across teams and products.

---

**Q44. Correct: A**

Responsible data handling requires de-identifying or redacting PII and validating regulatory and policy compliance (e.g., data protection laws, internal privacy policy) before using customer data for model training, enabling progress while managing risk.

---

**Q45. Correct: C**

In a high-stakes, legally sensitive decision domain, responsible AI practice requires a human-in-the-loop control so the non-deterministic model output augments rather than replaces the final decision, combined with logging and bias auditing to detect disparate impact — this preserves the value of the AI feature while managing legal and ethical risk.
