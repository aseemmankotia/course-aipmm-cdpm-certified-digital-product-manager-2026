**Q29. Correct: B**

Even though Feature A has a larger absolute Cost of Delay, Feature B's smaller effort produces a higher WSJF score, meaning it delivers more value per unit of time invested and should be sequenced first.

**Q31. Correct: D**

A retention curve that declines then flattens (a "smile curve") signals that a subset of users has found lasting value — this is a positive signal of product-market fit for that segment. The correct next step is to study these retained users (qualitatively and via segmentation) to understand what drove their stickiness, then apply those learnings to onboarding and activation for new users.

**Q32. Correct: B**

**Q33. Correct: A**

Guardrail metrics protect against a primary metric improving at the expense of overall business health. A checkout redesign could boost conversion by, for example, hiding shipping costs or simplifying disclosures — which could reduce AOV or spike refunds. Monitoring AOV and refund rate ensures a conversion 'win' isn't actually eroding revenue quality or customer trust.

**Q34. Correct: B**

Weekly activation rate by acquisition channel lets a PM identify which channels bring in users who actually experience value, enabling targeted action (e.g., reallocating spend or fixing onboarding for a specific channel).

**Q37. Correct: D**

Retention curve shape, not the initial activation number, is what signals product-market fit. A curve that flattens (asymptotes above zero) shows a durable, habituated user base — the classic PMF retention signal. A curve that keeps decaying toward zero shows users are churning out entirely regardless of initial activation. Because Month1 was identical for both cohorts, the divergence at Months 2-6 is the actionable insight, and the correct action is root-cause analysis of what differed between the cohorts (e.g., an onboarding change, a new activation feature) so the winning factor can be reinforced.

**Q38. Correct: C**

A blended conversion rate can drop mechanically when the free-tier funnel grows 40% with a mix of less-qualified, top-of-funnel users, even if conversion within any given acquisition cohort is stable or improving — this is a classic metric-dilution trap. The 118% NRR shows the monetization engine is healthy and capturing expansion value from existing accounts, and rising engagement suggests the product is delivering value. The disciplined action is to segment the metric (by cohort/acquisition source) to find the real signal before making any policy change, consistent with distinguishing a vanity/blended metric from an actionable, segmented one.

**Q39. Correct: D**

**Q40. Correct: A**

When the cost of a false negative (missed harmful content) is much higher than a false positive, the PM should tune the model toward recall on the harmful class and offset the resulting increase in false positives with a lightweight appeals/override path, which is a standard responsible-AI mitigation for asymmetric error costs.

**Q41. Correct: C**

When data volume is small, patterns are well understood, and full explainability is a compliance requirement, a rules-based system is the right initial approach: it needs no training data, is fully auditable, and matches the known patterns exactly. ML should be reserved for cases where patterns are complex, data volume is sufficient, and some accuracy-for-explainability tradeoff is acceptable.

---

**Q42. Correct: C**

Standard API governance practice for breaking changes is explicit versioning plus a published deprecation/sunset policy with advance notice, so integrators can migrate on their own schedule without unplanned outages.

---

**Q43. Correct: D**

Repurposing personal data for a new use (training an AI model) beyond its original collection purpose is a core privacy/purpose-limitation issue. The PM must confirm the legal basis or consent covers this secondary use and drive de-identification/anonymization or re-consent before engineering work proceeds, since this is a compliance blocker, not a technical detail.

---

**Q44. Correct: D**

For high-stakes, regulated decisions like credit approval, responsible-AI practice calls for human-in-the-loop review with explainable contributing factors and an audit trail of agreement/override, supporting both accountability and compliance with fair-lending and adverse-action disclosure requirements.

---

**Q45. Correct: A**

For high-stakes, irreversible, regulated decisions, responsible AI practice calls for human-in-the-loop review, decision logging, and explainability so adverse outcomes can be justified and audited (supporting fair-lending compliance and adverse-action-notice requirements) while still capturing the ML model's speed and pattern-detection value as a recommendation rather than a final autonomous verdict.
