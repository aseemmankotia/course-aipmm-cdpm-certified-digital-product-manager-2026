## Chapter 9: Growth Experimentation, Pricing, and Product-Led Growth — Practice Questions

**Q1.** A B2B SaaS product shows: Signup 10,000 → Activation 6,000 → First Value Moment 1,200 → Paid Conversion 900. Which stage should the team prioritize for a growth experiment?

A) Signup to Activation (60% conversion)
B) First Value Moment to Paid Conversion (75% conversion)
C) Activation to First Value Moment (20% conversion)
D) Add more top-of-funnel acquisition spend

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Activation to First Value has the lowest conversion rate (20%, an 80% drop), making it the biggest leak and highest-leverage stage to fix before adding volume elsewhere.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- 60% conversion is comparatively healthy; not the priority leak.
- 75% conversion is the strongest stage in the funnel and needs no urgent fix.
- Increasing top-of-funnel volume amplifies losses at the existing 80% drop-off instead of fixing it.

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** Which characteristic best distinguishes a product-led growth (PLG) motion from a sales-led motion?

A) PLG relies on outbound SDRs booking demos before any product access is granted
B) PLG only works for enterprise contracts above a high annual contract value
C) Sales-led motions always have lower customer acquisition cost than PLG
D) In PLG, users can experience core product value and self-serve to purchase without talking to a salesperson

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

PLG is defined by end users self-serving through activation and purchase, using the product itself as the primary driver of acquisition, conversion, and expansion.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Outbound-first demo booking before product access describes a sales-led motion, the inverse of PLG.
- PLG is typically associated with lower-touch, lower-ACV segments, not exclusively large enterprise deals.
- Sales-led motions generally carry higher CAC due to rep and SDR/AE compensation and longer cycles.

</details>

**Q3.** A developer-tools API company charges customers strictly based on the number of API calls consumed each month, with no fixed monthly fee. Which pricing model is this?

A) Flat-rate pricing
B) Tiered pricing
C) Freemium pricing
D) Usage-based (consumption) pricing

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Charging directly per unit of consumption (API calls) with no fixed fee is the defining trait of usage-based pricing.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Flat-rate implies one fixed price regardless of consumption, which contradicts the scenario.
- Tiered pricing uses fixed bands with bundled limits/features, not pure per-call billing.
- Freemium requires a free tier alongside paid tiers, which is not described here.

</details>

**Q4.** A freemium product increases free-tier project limits from 3 to 10. Over the next month, free-to-paid conversion drops from 8% to 3% while free signups rise 40%. What is the correct next action?

A) Test reintroducing a meaningful usage cap or gating a high-value feature behind the paid tier
B) Assume the pricing page is broken and roll back the change immediately
C) Ignore the conversion drop since total signups increased
D) Immediately raise the price of the paid tier to offset lost revenue

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Widening the free tier removed the usage-limit friction that previously drove upgrades; the correct fix is to reintroduce a meaningful constraint or value gate tied to willingness to pay, then re-test.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- There is no evidence of a technical bug; the pattern matches a removed upgrade trigger, not a broken page.
- Ignoring the conversion drop treats signup growth as success while revenue-driving conversion is degrading.
- Raising price without addressing the removed upgrade trigger does not fix the root cause and may worsen conversion further.

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q5.** Which of the following best describes an effective in-product upgrade prompt in a PLG model?

A) A prompt shown to all users immediately after signup, before any value has been experienced
B) A prompt sent only via a monthly email newsletter
C) A prompt that requires scheduling a sales call before any pricing is shown
D) A prompt triggered when a user hits a usage limit or attempts to access a gated feature they're actively seeking

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Effective PLG upgrade prompts are contextual and value-tied, appearing at the moment a user's need exceeds the free tier's capability.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Prompting before any value is experienced ignores context and typically has poor conversion.
- Email-only prompts are not in-product and lack contextual relevance.
- Requiring a sales call before showing pricing contradicts the self-serve nature of PLG.

</details>

**Q6.** A PM designs an A/B test adding a guided setup checklist to reduce drop-off between activation and first value moment. Which is the correct primary success metric?

A) Overall company revenue growth quarter-over-quarter
B) Percentage of activated users who reach the first value moment within 7 days
C) Number of signups originating from paid ad campaigns
D) Net Promoter Score collected 90 days after signup

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

The success metric must be tied directly to the funnel stage under test — activation-to-first-value conversion within a defined window.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Quarterly revenue is a lagging, broad metric not isolated to this funnel stage or experiment.
- Ad-driven signup volume measures top-of-funnel acquisition, unrelated to this experiment's stage.
- NPS at 90 days is both the wrong stage and the wrong timeframe for this specific test.

</details>

**Q7.** A project management tool serves solo users needing basic task lists, small teams needing collaboration, and enterprises needing SSO and admin controls. Which pricing model best fits this product?

A) Pure usage-based pricing charged per action taken
B) Flat-rate pricing with a single price for all customers
C) Freemium with no paid tiers, monetized only through ads
D) Tiered pricing with feature-gated tiers aligned to each segment (e.g., Free/Team/Enterprise)

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Tiered pricing lets each segment pay for the feature set and limits relevant to its needs, matching willingness to pay across solo, team, and enterprise users.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Per-action usage pricing doesn't map cleanly to feature needs like SSO or admin controls.
- A single flat price ignores the clear value differences across the three segments described.
- An ad-only freemium model doesn't fit a B2B collaboration tool with enterprise requirements.

</details>

**Q8.** Trial-to-paid conversion has stayed flat at 15% for six months despite three rounds of onboarding experiments, even though time-in-app during trial rose 25%. What is the most likely correct next action?

A) Run additional onboarding experiments similar to the previous three
B) Investigate whether pricing or packaging itself is the blocker, e.g., via pricing page tests or customer interviews
C) Conclude the product has no growth potential and shift entirely to a sales-led model
D) Shorten the trial length to force faster purchase decisions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Rising engagement with flat conversion after repeated onboarding fixes signals the bottleneck has moved past activation — likely to pricing, packaging, or perceived value — warranting investigation at that stage.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Repeating similar onboarding experiments has already shown diminishing returns across three rounds.
- Shifting entirely to sales-led is an overreaction not supported by the data, which points to a specific funnel stage, not total product failure.
- Shortening the trial doesn't address why engaged users still aren't converting and could reduce conversion further.

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>
