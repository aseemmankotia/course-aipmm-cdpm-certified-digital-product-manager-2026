## Chapter 9: Growth Experimentation, Pricing, and Product-Led Growth — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | How do you identify the biggest drop-off stage in a funnel A→B→C→D? | Calculate the conversion rate between each consecutive stage (stage_n / stage_n-1). The stage with the lowest % conversion is the priority for experimentation — not the stage with the largest raw number lost. |
| 2 | PLG core mechanic: self-serve | Users can sign up, activate, and pay without talking to a salesperson. Exam tip: 'no sales rep required to purchase' signals PLG. |
| 3 | Freemium vs. Free Trial — key difference | Freemium: no time limit, gated by feature/usage caps. Free Trial: full-featured but time-boxed, access ends unless the user pays. Exam tip: 'time-limited' = trial; 'usage-capped, unlimited time' = freemium. |
| 4 | Flat-rate pricing model — definition and weakness | One price for all customers regardless of usage or tier. Simple, but fails to capture more value from high-usage customers or serve varied segments. |
| 5 | Usage-based (consumption) pricing — best fit | Price scales with metered usage (API calls, GB stored). Best for infrastructure/API/dev-tools products where usage correlates directly with value delivered. |
| 6 | Tiered pricing — best fit | Fixed price bands (e.g., Free/Pro/Enterprise) bundling features and limits. Best for products serving distinct customer segments with different needs. |
| 7 | When should an in-product upgrade prompt trigger? | At the moment a user hits friction tied to value — a usage limit or a gated feature they're actively trying to use. Exam trap: prompting immediately at signup before value is experienced is the common wrong answer. |
| 8 | PLG motion vs. Sales-led motion — who initiates the buying process? | PLG: end user experiences product value first, then self-serve purchases or expands (bottom-up). Sales-led: a rep engages a buyer before product access (top-down). |
| 9 | What must a valid growth experiment define before launch? | A single primary success metric tied to the specific funnel stage being targeted, a clear hypothesis, and a defined sample size/duration — not a broad lagging metric like total revenue. |
| 10 | Engagement up but conversion flat after repeated onboarding experiments — correct next action? | Stop iterating on the same lever (onboarding); investigate the next funnel stage or root cause (e.g., pricing/packaging). Exam trap: 'run more onboarding tests' is the common wrong answer. |
| 11 | Sales-led motion — typical cost characteristic | Sales-led (outbound/enterprise) motions typically carry higher CAC due to SDR/AE compensation and longer sales cycles, offset by higher ACV per deal. Don't assume sales-led always means lower CAC. |
| 12 | Freemium conversion drops after widening free-tier limits — root cause pattern | Removing the usage cap removes the friction point that drove upgrades, so conversion falls even if signups/engagement rise. Fix: reintroduce a meaningful cap or gate a high-value feature, not just add more free value. |
