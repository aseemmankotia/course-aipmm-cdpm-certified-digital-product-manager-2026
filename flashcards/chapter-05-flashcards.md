## Chapter 5: MVP Types and Experiment Design — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Concierge MVP vs Wizard of Oz MVP — key difference? | Concierge: human delivers the service manually and openly (customer knows it's manual). Wizard of Oz: human does the work behind the scenes while the customer believes it's automated. Exam tip: 'customer is told the truth' = Concierge; 'customer is deceived about automation' = Wizard of Oz. |
| 2 | What does a landing page / smoke test MVP validate? | Pure demand signal (sign-ups, waitlist, pre-orders, click-through) before any product or service is built. Cheapest MVP option when the riskiest assumption is 'will anyone want this?' |
| 3 | When is a Single-feature MVP the right choice? | When a working product already exists and the team wants to test adoption of one specific new capability, without rebuilding the whole product or faking a backend. |
| 4 | Conventional statistical significance threshold in A/B testing? | p < 0.05 — conventionally means the observed difference is unlikely to be due to chance. Not a guarantee the effect is large or business-relevant. |
| 5 | What must every A/B test include besides the variant being tested? | A control group — the baseline (existing) experience — so the variant's performance can be compared against something. |
| 6 | When should the success metric for an experiment be defined? | Before launch. Defining it (and its pass/fail threshold) up front prevents post-hoc cherry-picking of a flattering metric after results come in. |
| 7 | Exam trap: a stripped-down version of the full product with real infrastructure, accounts, and payments launched to the whole market — is it an MVP? | No. This is a scoped-down full release mislabeled as an MVP. A true MVP tests the riskiest assumption at minimum cost/time — feature count alone doesn't define it. |
| 8 | Cheapest valid MVP type to test pure demand before building anything? | Landing page / smoke test MVP — no product, no manual service, no fake backend required; just a demand signal. |
| 9 | Concierge MVP — classic example? | Founder manually plans and delivers a personalized service for early customers (e.g., manually curating meal plans), openly acting as the 'concierge' rather than software. |
| 10 | Wizard of Oz MVP — classic example? | A 'smart' app front-end where a human manually processes requests behind the scenes, while the user believes an algorithm is doing the work automatically. |
| 11 | What three factors determine required sample size in an A/B test? | Baseline conversion rate, minimum detectable effect size, and desired statistical power (commonly 80%). Smaller effects or lower baseline rates require larger sample sizes. |
| 12 | How to distinguish a genuine MVP from a mislabeled first release? | Ask: was this built at minimum cost/time specifically to test one risky assumption, or was it a scaled-down version of the final planned product built to confirm an existing plan? The latter is not an MVP regardless of feature count. |
