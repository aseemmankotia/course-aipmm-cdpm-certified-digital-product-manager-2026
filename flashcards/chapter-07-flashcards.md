## Chapter 7: Prioritization Frameworks and Outcome-Based Roadmapping — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | RICE score formula? | RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort. Exam tip: Confidence is a decimal (0–1), not a whole percentage — using 80 instead of 0.8 inflates the score 100x. |
| 2 | WSJF formula? | WSJF = Cost of Delay / Job Size (Duration). Cost of Delay = User-Business Value + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction/Opportunity Enablement Value. Exam tip: higher WSJF score = deliver first. |
| 3 | When to choose RICE vs WSJF on the exam? | RICE: limited/uncertain data, large backlog of feature ideas — Confidence factor absorbs uncertainty. WSJF: high cost-of-delay urgency, economic sequencing across competing initiatives (common in SAFe scenarios). |
| 4 | Now-Next-Later roadmap structure? | Now = active work, high confidence, short-term. Next = upcoming priorities, medium confidence. Later = exploratory/strategic direction, low confidence, no fixed dates. Exam tip: outcome-based, avoids overcommitting to dates. |
| 5 | Why does the exam favor Now-Next-Later over a date-based roadmap? | Date-based roadmaps create false certainty and damage stakeholder trust when estimates slip. Now-Next-Later communicates direction and sequencing without locking in delivery dates. |
| 6 | Three core Kano feature categories tested on the exam? | Basic (must-be): absence causes dissatisfaction, presence doesn't raise it above baseline. Performance: linear — more delivered = more satisfaction. Delighter: unexpected, boosts satisfaction, absence doesn't cause dissatisfaction. |
| 7 | MoSCoW categories and their meaning? | Must have (non-negotiable), Should have (important but not vital), Could have (nice-to-have, first cut under constraint), Won't have (explicitly out of scope this release). Exam tip: used to negotiate scope under a fixed deadline/capacity. |
| 8 | What does value stream mapping identify in delivery flow? | Bottlenecks and non-value-add steps between idea and delivered value, by comparing lead time vs cycle time at each stage. Exam tip: used to find where work sits idle, not to prioritize backlog items. |
| 9 | What does 'Effort' represent in RICE, and how does it affect the score? | Total person-time (e.g., person-months) needed to deliver the feature. It's the denominator — higher effort lowers the RICE score even when reach/impact/confidence are high. |
| 10 | Three components that sum to Cost of Delay in WSJF? | User-Business Value + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction & Opportunity Enablement Value. Each is typically scored on a relative scale before summing. |
| 11 | Scenario: single release, fixed date, need stakeholder scope agreement — MoSCoW or RICE? | MoSCoW. Designed for binary in/out scope negotiation under a fixed timebox, unlike RICE which ranks a large multi-quarter backlog. |
| 12 | Exam trap: why can't you rely only on delighters long-term? | Delighters shift into performance or basic expectations over time as users get accustomed to them (Kano category migration). Common mistake: assuming a delighter differentiates the product indefinitely. |
