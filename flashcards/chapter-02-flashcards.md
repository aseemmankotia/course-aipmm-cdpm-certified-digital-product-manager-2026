## Chapter 2: Customer Discovery, JTBD, and Design Thinking — Understanding the User — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the three job types in JTBD theory? | Functional (practical task to accomplish), Emotional (how the customer wants to feel), Social (how the customer wants to be perceived by others). Exam tip: interview quotes about feelings = emotional; quotes about others' perception = social. |
| 2 | What is the exact job story format? | 'When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].' Exam tip: no persona/role appears in a job story — that's the key differentiator from a user story. |
| 3 | What is the traditional user story format? | 'As a [role/persona], I want [goal], so that [benefit].' Exam tip: user stories center on WHO the user is; job stories center on the SITUATION triggering the need. |
| 4 | List the 5 Design Thinking stages in order. | 1) Empathize 2) Define 3) Ideate 4) Prototype 5) Test. Exam tip: this exact order is heavily tested — Define always comes right after Empathize, before any idea generation. |
| 5 | Job story vs user story — what's the key structural difference? | Job story emphasizes the triggering situation ('When...'), no persona attached. User story emphasizes the actor/role ('As a...'). Exam tip: watch for trick questions that mislabel a role-based sentence as a 'job story'. |
| 6 | Personas vs jobs-based segmentation — what does each group by? | Personas group customers by WHO they are (demographics, attitudes, behaviors). Jobs-based segmentation groups customers by the progress/job they're trying to make, regardless of who they are. |
| 7 | In Design Thinking, what happens during 'Define'? | The team synthesizes empathy research into a clear, actionable problem statement (often a point-of-view or 'How Might We' statement). Exam tip: Define is about FRAMING the problem, not proposing solutions. |
| 8 | In Design Thinking, what happens during 'Ideate'? | Generating a broad range of possible solutions/ideas after the problem has been clearly defined. Exam tip: Ideate cannot come before Define — an exam trap is reordering these two stages. |
| 9 | What is 'problem framing before solutioning'? | The discipline of clearly defining the underlying customer problem/job BEFORE proposing or building a feature. Exam tip: if a scenario jumps straight to a feature request without validating the underlying need, that's a red flag / wrong answer choice. |
| 10 | What does journey mapping surface? | Friction points and emotional highs/lows across a customer's end-to-end experience with a product or service. Exam tip: discovery/ideation effort should be prioritized at the stage(s) with the highest identified friction, not evenly across all stages. |
| 11 | How do you choose a discovery method for high uncertainty + low resources/time? | Favor small-batch qualitative methods (5-8 interviews) to explore the 'why' before investing in quantitative validation (surveys, A/B tests) or building prototypes. Exam tip: method choice should match the level of uncertainty — qualitative for exploring unknowns, quantitative for validating known hypotheses. |
| 12 | Emotional job example vs Social job example — how to tell them apart in interview quotes? | Emotional: 'I want to feel confident/not embarrassed' (internal feeling). Social: 'I want my team to see me as organized' (external perception). Exam tip: look for the word 'feel' (emotional) vs. reference to other people's opinion (social). |
