## Chapter 10: UX Fundamentals, Accessibility, and Design Systems for PMs — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | WCAG 2.1 Level AA minimum contrast ratio for normal text? | 4.5:1. Exam tip: 3:1 applies only to large text (18pt+ or 14pt bold+). |
| 2 | WCAG 2.1 Level AA minimum contrast ratio for large text? | 3:1. Large text = 18pt normal weight or 14pt bold. Exam tip: don't confuse with the 4.5:1 normal-text rule. |
| 3 | WCAG Level AAA contrast ratio for normal text? | 7:1. Stricter than AA (4.5:1); most products target AA, not AAA, unless mandated. |
| 4 | How many users does Nielsen's research say are needed to find ~85% of usability issues? | 5 users. Exam tip: this is the standard justification for small, fast PM-led usability tests. |
| 5 | WCAG Success Criterion 2.1.1 | All functionality must be operable through a keyboard interface alone. Exam tip: custom-built widgets (divs styled as buttons/dropdowns) commonly fail this. |
| 6 | WCAG Success Criterion 1.1.1 | Non-text content (images, icons) must have text alternatives (alt text). Exam tip: screen readers rely on this to convey meaning. |
| 7 | Nielsen heuristic: Visibility of system status | System must give timely, appropriate feedback on what's happening (e.g., loading spinners, confirmations). Exam trap: silent delays after user actions. |
| 8 | Nielsen heuristic: Error prevention | Design to prevent problems before they occur, better than good error messages after the fact. Exam tip: confirmation dialogs for destructive actions. |
| 9 | Nielsen heuristic: Consistency and standards | Follow platform/industry conventions so users don't have to learn new meanings. Exam tip: violated when custom components ignore established patterns without reason. |
| 10 | When should a PM request custom design instead of reusing a design system component? | Only when the existing component cannot meet a genuine functional or accessibility requirement. Exam tip: brand preference alone is NOT sufficient justification. |
| 11 | Design system reuse vs custom design — default choice? | Default to reuse: it preserves consistency, is already accessibility-vetted, and reduces cost/time. Custom is the exception, not the rule. |
| 12 | PM's role in interaction design details (e.g., exact animation timing, micro-interactions)? | Partner with design/engineering — PM should not own or dictate these details directly. Exam tip: PM owns requirements and validation, not pixel-level interaction specs. |
