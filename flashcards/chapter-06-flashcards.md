## Chapter 6: Agile Delivery Fundamentals for Digital Products — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Product Owner vs Scrum Master vs Dev Team — who owns backlog prioritization? | Product Owner. Scrum Master facilitates process/removes impediments; Dev Team owns how work gets done technically. Exam tip: 'decision rights' questions test this split constantly. |
| 2 | Standard user story template | 'As a [role], I want [goal], so that [benefit].' Exam tip: missing the 'so that' (value/benefit) clause is a common story-writing flaw tested on exams. |
| 3 | INVEST acronym — what does each letter stand for? | Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. Exam tip: stories that bundle multiple features violate 'Independent' and 'Small.' |
| 4 | Acceptance criteria vs Definition of Done — key difference | Acceptance criteria = story-specific conditions for that item only. Definition of Done = team-wide standard (tested, reviewed, deployed) applied to every increment. Exam tip: these are frequently confused in trap questions. |
| 5 | Given/When/Then format — what is it used for? | Writing testable acceptance criteria: Given [context], When [action], Then [expected outcome]. Exam tip: recognize this as acceptance criteria, not Definition of Done. |
| 6 | Recommended time budget for backlog refinement per sprint | Typically ~5-10% of total sprint capacity. Exam tip: skipping refinement causes long, chaotic sprint planning and unclear/rejected stories. |
| 7 | Story points vs T-shirt sizing — when to use which | T-shirt sizing (S/M/L/XL): fast, rough sizing for large/unrefined backlogs or new teams with no velocity history. Story points: relative sizing once a team has a calibrated reference story and some historical velocity. |
| 8 | Scrum vs Kanban — best fit for unpredictable, continuous work (e.g., support tickets)? | Kanban — uses WIP limits and continuous flow, releases as items complete, no fixed iteration commitment. Scrum fits planned, batchable work with fixed sprint commitments. |
| 9 | Who is accountable for ensuring a story meets Definition of Ready before sprint planning? | Product Owner. Exam tip: a story that's 'too vague to estimate' at planning time is flagged as a PO/refinement failure, not a dev team failure. |
| 10 | Technical debt visibility — what is the PM-level responsibility? | PM/PO should keep technical debt items visible in the backlog (not hidden in a separate 'engineering-only' list) and periodically allocate capacity to address it, since unmanaged debt slows future delivery. Exam tip: 'invisible' tech debt is a common scenario trap answer. |
| 11 | Kanban WIP limits — purpose | Cap the number of items allowed in a workflow stage at once to expose bottlenecks and improve flow/cycle time. Exam tip: WIP limits are a defining Kanban practice absent from core Scrum. |
| 12 | Velocity — definition and common exam trap | The average amount of story points a team completes per sprint, used for forecasting. Exam trap: velocity is team-specific and cannot be reliably compared or transferred across different teams. |
