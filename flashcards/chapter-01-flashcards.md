## Chapter 1: Digital Product Management Foundations — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Outcome vs output — core exam distinction? | Output = what was shipped (features, story points, releases). Outcome = the resulting change in customer/business behavior (conversion, retention, revenue). Exam favors answers that measure outcomes. |
| 2 | Project team vs persistent product team — key difference? | Project team: fixed scope/timeline, disbands after launch. Persistent product team: cross-functional, stays accountable for the product across its full life cycle. |
| 3 | Continuous delivery vs one-time launch — how do they treat 'launch day'? | Continuous delivery: launch = start of an ongoing iteration loop. One-time launch: launch = end of the initiative/project. |
| 4 | Vision-to-strategy cascade order (abstract → concrete) | Vision → Strategy → Roadmap → Backlog. Exam tests recognizing this exact sequence and identifying misordered options. |
| 5 | Digital PM role across phases — Discovery focus? | Validating problems/solutions with users via interviews and prototypes, before committing to build. |
| 6 | Digital PM role across phases — Delivery focus? | Shipping the validated solution: prioritizing backlog, coordinating build, managing scope/quality tradeoffs. |
| 7 | Digital PM role across phases — Growth focus? | Scaling adoption of an already-launched product: experimentation, optimization, retention/expansion metrics. |
| 8 | Platform product — defining trait? | Provides shared capabilities/infrastructure that other products or teams build on top of (enablement is the core value). |
| 9 | Data product — defining trait? | The dataset itself (curated, trustworthy, discoverable, accessible) IS the primary value delivered, regardless of interface. |
| 10 | Signal that a product has entered maturity/decline (sunset) stage | Flat or falling usage, rising support/maintenance cost, no planned feature investment. Operating stance shifts to minimal maintenance + retirement/migration planning. |
| 11 | Common exam trap: 'the team hit all sprint commitments' as proof of success | This is an OUTPUT metric (delivery predictability), not proof of OUTCOME (business/customer impact). Exam marks this as insufficient evidence alone. |
| 12 | Choosing operating stance — new, unvalidated 0-to-1 product idea | Favor Discovery-phase activity and, if scope/timeline is fixed and finite, a project team may be acceptable temporarily; but sustained product-market-fit pursuit favors a persistent team once validated. |
