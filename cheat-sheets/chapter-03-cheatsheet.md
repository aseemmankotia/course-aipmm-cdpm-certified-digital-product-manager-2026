## Chapter 3 Cheat Sheet: Continuous Discovery and Opportunity Framing

### Opportunity-Solution Tree Structure (heavily tested)

| Level | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome (root) | Single measurable goal the product trio owns | Increase trial-to-paid conversion |
| Opportunities | Customer needs, pain points, or friction that could drive the outcome | "Users can't tell which plan fits their team size" |
| Solutions | Potential ways to address an opportunity | In-app plan comparison tool |
| Experiments | Small tests to validate a solution before full build | Prototype test with 5 users |

**Rule:** Every node must trace upward to the single outcome. Never add a solution without a parent opportunity.

### Continuous Discovery Cadence (heavily tested)

| Habit | Frequency | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Customer interview | Weekly (minimum) | Product trio (PM, designer, engineer) |
| Opportunity tree review | Ongoing, updated as evidence arrives | Product trio |
| Assumption testing | Continuous, before major build commitment | Product trio |

**Anti-pattern:** Quarterly/annual "big bang" research studies are NOT continuous discovery — they lack the ongoing, weekly rhythm.

### Validated vs. Assumed Problem (heavily tested)

| Signal | Validated | Assumed |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Direct customer interviews/observed behavior | Internal opinion, stakeholder request, guesswork |
| Evidence pattern | Repeated across multiple independent interviews | Single anecdote or no interview at all |
| Statement form | Describes friction/need, no solution named | Often already names a solution |

### Assumption Mapping — 3 Types

| Type | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Desirability | Will customers want/use this? |
| Feasibility | Can we build/support this? |
| Viability | Does the value exceed the cost/business risk? |

### Turning Insight into a Testable Problem Statement
- Bad: "Users need a dashboard with real-time analytics" (names a solution)
- Good: "Users can't quickly determine which metrics matter before making a decision" (describes friction, solution-agnostic)

### Journey Map → Opportunity Source
Friction point observed in journey map → becomes an **Opportunity** node → explored via interviews → THEN solutions considered. Never skip straight from friction to solution.

### Avoiding Solution-First Bias
- Red flag: designing/building before validating the opportunity
- Red flag: seeking interviews only to confirm an already-chosen solution
- Fix: always frame and validate the opportunity node before adding solution nodes

### Selecting an Opportunity (Limited Bandwidth)
Prioritize by:
1. Strength of customer evidence (multiple interviews confirming pain)
2. Alignment to the outcome metric
Do NOT prioritize by: stakeholder loudness, engineering ease alone, or attempting all opportunities simultaneously.
