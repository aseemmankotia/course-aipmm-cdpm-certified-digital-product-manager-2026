## Chapter 2 Cheat Sheet: Customer Discovery, JTBD & Design Thinking

### JTBD: Three Job Types
| Job Type | Definition | Interview Clue |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | Practical task the customer needs done | 'I need to...' |
| Emotional | How the customer wants to feel (or avoid feeling) | 'I felt like a failure...' |
| Social | How the customer wants to be perceived by others | 'My team would think...' |

### Job Story vs. User Story
| | Job Story | User Story |
|---|---|---|
| Format | When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome] | As a [role], I want [goal], so that [benefit] |
| Focus | Triggering situation | Persona/role |
| Persona required? | No | Yes |

### Design Thinking — 5 Stages (ORDER IS TESTED)
1. **Empathize** — research users, observe, interview
2. **Define** — synthesize into a clear problem statement
3. **Ideate** — generate broad range of solutions
4. **Prototype** — build low/high-fidelity representation
5. **Test** — validate with real users, iterate

> Exam trap: questions reorder Define and Ideate, or place Prototype before Ideate. Memory trick: **E-D-I-P-T** ('Every Designer Ideates, Prototypes, Tests').

### Personas vs. Jobs-Based Segmentation
| | Personas | Jobs-Based Segmentation |
|---|---|---|
| Groups by | Who the user is (demographics, attitudes) | Progress the user is trying to make |
| Risk | Can mask shared jobs across different demographics | Requires strong qualitative research to define jobs accurately |

### Journey Mapping
- Plots stages of a customer's experience with associated **emotion/friction** at each touchpoint.
- Use to **prioritize** discovery/ideation at the highest-friction stage — not spread evenly.

### Problem Framing Before Solutioning
- Never accept a stakeholder's feature request at face value.
- First ask: *What underlying job/friction does this solve?*
- Exam pattern: correct answer usually reframes toward research/validation; wrong answers jump straight to build/scope.

### Choosing a Discovery Method by Uncertainty Level
| Uncertainty | Best Method |
|---|---|
| High (don't know the 'why') | 5–8 qualitative interviews |
| Medium (have a hypothesis) | Survey at scale |
| Low (know the problem, testing a fix) | A/B test or usability test on prototype |

> Exam trap: choosing A/B testing or large surveys when the team has *zero* understanding of root cause — qualitative research must come first.
